Massage is one of the most searched wellness treats in Australia, and for good reason. An hour on the table can undo a week of hunched shoulders. Sound baths are newer to most people, but they're growing fast. Both promise deep relaxation. They just get there by different doors.

The Short Answer

If your stress lives mainly in tight, sore muscles, book a massage. If your stress lives mainly in a busy mind that won't switch off, try a sound bath. If you want the calm to last beyond the session, add a few minutes of daily meditation. That's the part that changes how you handle stress over time.

What a Massage Does Well

Massage loosens tight muscles, and research suggests it can lower stress and anxiety in the short term. Part of the magic is simply being cared for: an hour with no phone, someone else's slow, steady hands, and permission to do nothing. If your neck and back carry your stress, it's hard to beat.

The limits are cost and time. Most people can't have one every week, and the relaxed feeling usually fades within a day or two.

What a Sound Bath Does Well

In a sound bath you lie on a mat, close your eyes and let the sound wash over you. The long, fading tones of singing bowls give a busy mind something gentle to follow, so thoughts slow down without you forcing them. Many people drift into a state between awake and asleep. Some fall asleep completely, which is fine.

Research on sound baths is still young, but early studies suggest people feel less tense and anxious afterwards. It's also great for anyone who doesn't love being touched, or who finds sitting meditation hard.

Sound baths can feel intense for some people. If you have epilepsy, a pacemaker, sensitive hearing, or are pregnant, talk to your doctor first and tell the practitioner before you start. You can always sit up, move further from the instruments or step out.

Which Should You Try?

  • Sore back, tight shoulders, after sport: massage.
  • Racing thoughts, poor sleep, feeling "wired": sound bath.
  • Don't like being touched: sound bath.
  • Want to claim it on health insurance: remedial massage.
  • Want something you can do at home for free: a singing bowl and a few minutes of quiet. See how to use a singing bowl.

Making the Calm Last

Both a massage and a sound bath are lovely resets. Neither one teaches you to calm yourself on a stressful Tuesday afternoon. A short daily practice does. Frequently Asked Questions

Is a sound bath better than a massage?

Not better, different. Massage works best for muscle tension. A sound bath works best for a busy mind and for people who'd rather not be touched. Many people enjoy both.

What does a sound bath feel like compared with a massage?

A massage feels physical: pressure, warmth, muscles letting go. A sound bath feels more like drifting. You stay dressed and still, and the sound slowly quiets your thoughts.

Can I claim a sound bath on private health insurance?

Usually not. Remedial massage from a registered therapist is often covered under extras. Check your own policy.

Can you fall asleep in a sound bath?

Yes, and many people do. It's a sign your body needed the rest.
