Most beginners are told to "focus on the breath" and then spend ten minutes thinking about dinner. A singing bowl gives you something much easier to hold on to: a sound that starts clearly and slowly fades away. Following it all the way to silence takes just enough attention to quiet the chatter, without any effort to "empty your mind".

You don't need to own a bowl to start. Use the free singing bowl player (it loops my own bowl for 5 to 30 minutes), or the guided video further down this page.

Why Singing Bowls Help You Meditate

  • A clear anchor. The sound is right there. You don't have to search for it the way you search for a subtle breath.
  • A built-in rhythm. Each strike rises and fades, which naturally slows your breathing to match.
  • A gentle way back. When your mind wanders, the next strike calls you back without any sense of failure.
  • A ritual. Striking a bowl to begin and end tells your body "this is quiet time", and that signal gets stronger every day you use it.

Singing bowl meditation is a relaxation and awareness practice, not a medical treatment. If you're dealing with anxiety, depression or sleep problems, it can sit alongside professional care but shouldn't replace it.

The 10-Minute Singing Bowl Meditation

Read it through once, then try it. If you're using our player, choose 10 minutes and press Begin.

1. Arrive (1 minute)

Sit on a chair or cushion with your back upright and your hands resting in your lap. Close your eyes. Take three slow breaths: in through the nose, and a longer, softer breath out. Let your shoulders drop.

2. Follow the first sound (2 minutes)

Strike the bowl once, or let the recording begin. Listen to the very start of the tone, its shimmer, and how it slowly gets quieter. Try to catch the exact moment the sound disappears. When it's gone, strike again.

3. Let the sound move through your body (3 minutes)

Keep listening, but now also notice where you feel the sound. It might be your chest, your face or your hands. You don't need to feel anything special. Just notice what's there. Each time the sound fades, take one slow breath before the next strike.

4. Notice the silence (3 minutes)

Now pay attention to the gap after each sound ends. That quiet space is always there; the bowl just points to it. When a thought appears, notice it, and let the next sound bring you back. You're not trying to stop thoughts, just to notice them sooner.

5. Close (1 minute)

Strike the bowl one last time and stay until it fades completely. Feel your feet on the floor. Open your eyes slowly and take a moment before you stand up.

When Your Mind Won't Stop

It will wander, many times. That's normal, and it's not a sign you're doing it wrong. The practice isn't keeping your attention still; it's noticing that it moved and coming back. Every time you come back is one "rep".

  • Too restless? Strike more often, every 10 to 15 seconds instead of waiting for silence.
  • Getting sleepy? Sit more upright and keep your eyes slightly open, looking at the bowl.
  • Feeling emotional? That happens sometimes when you slow down. Let it be there, breathe, and stop if you need to.

Making It a Daily Habit

Pick a time you already have, like after your morning coffee or before bed, and keep the bowl where you'll see it. Start with five minutes. If you miss a day, just start again the next. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need my own singing bowl?

No. A recording works well, especially with headphones. Owning a bowl adds the ritual of playing it yourself. Learn the basics in how to use a singing bowl.

How is this different from a sound bath?

In this meditation you play or follow one bowl and actively pay attention. In a sound bath you lie down and someone else plays many instruments around you.

What's the best time of day?

Whatever time you'll actually stick to. Mornings set the tone for the day; evenings help you unwind before sleep.
