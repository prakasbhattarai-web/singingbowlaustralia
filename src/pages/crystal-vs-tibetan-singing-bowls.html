If you've been to a sound bath lately, you've probably heard both. The big white bowls that seem to fill the whole room with one pure note are crystal bowls. The smaller, darker, slightly uneven ones that wobble and hum are metal bowls, often called Tibetan or Himalayan singing bowls. Both are lovely. They're just very different instruments.

The Short Answer

Choose a metal (Tibetan) bowl if you want something to hold, play daily and keep for life. Choose a crystal bowl if you love a strong, pure tone and mostly play in one spot at home or in a group. For a first bowl, most people do well with a medium metal bowl. It's forgiving, sturdy and easy to learn on.

How Crystal Bowls Sound

A crystal bowl is very even, so it produces one strong, steady note. Rub the rim with a suede mallet and the sound builds until the bowl seems to sing on its own. In a quiet room it's striking. Some people find it deeply calming. Others find it a little too much at close range, so sit a metre or two away the first time.

Because each crystal bowl gives one main note, practitioners often buy them in sets tuned to a scale. That's part of why they're so common in group sound baths.

How Tibetan Bowls Sound

A metal bowl is rarely perfectly even, and that's the charm. Strike it and you hear a low hum, a brighter ring and a gentle wobble as the tones drift in and out of each other. It's quieter and more complex than crystal, which makes it easy to listen to for a long time.

You can also hold a small or medium metal bowl in your open hand, which many people love for meditation. You feel the vibration as well as hear it. If you'd like to hear one now, try the player on the home page.

Which Is Better for Meditation?

Neither is "better". What matters is that you enjoy the sound enough to come back to it. As a rough guide:

  • For a daily sit at home, a medium metal bowl is hard to beat. Strike it once, listen until the sound fades, then rest in the quiet.
  • For a calm, room-filling sound during a longer relaxation, a crystal bowl works beautifully.
  • For sound baths, practitioners often use both: crystal for big, steady tones and metal bowls for texture and closeness.

A Few Honest Notes

You'll see big claims online about bowls "tuned to chakras" or "healing frequencies". Sound can help you relax and settle your attention, and that alone is worth a lot. But there's little good research behind specific healing frequencies, so don't pay extra for that label alone. Buy the bowl whose sound you love.

Care tips: keep crystal bowls on their rubber ring and away from hard floors and children's reach, and never strike them hard. Wipe metal bowls with a dry, soft cloth. If you have a pacemaker, are pregnant, or have epilepsy, skip placing a bowl directly on your body and check with your doctor before trying it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are crystal singing bowls better than Tibetan bowls?

No, they're different. Crystal bowls give one strong, pure note and suit sound baths. Tibetan metal bowls give warmer, layered tones, are sturdier and suit daily, hand-held meditation.

Which singing bowl is best for beginners?

A medium metal (Tibetan-style) bowl, around 12 to 18 cm across. It's easy to play, hard to damage and works for both striking and rim playing.

Why are crystal singing bowls so loud?

They're very evenly made from quartz, so the vibration is strong and steady. That's why they carry so far and can feel intense up close.

Can crystal singing bowls break?

Yes. They can chip or crack if they're dropped, knocked against something hard or struck too firmly. Keep them on their rubber ring and play them gently.
