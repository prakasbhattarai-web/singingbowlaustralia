How to Use a Singing Bowl: A Beginner's Guide

Two ways to play, the three mistakes almost every beginner makes, and a five-minute practice you can start tonight.

By Prakash Bhattarai · 14 November 2026 · 5 min read

A singing bowl looks simple, and it is. But the first time most people try one, it either clunks, rattles or goes silent after a second. Here is how to get a clear, long sound, and then how to actually use that sound to quiet your mind. Everything in this guide works with any metal singing bowl, whether it came from Nepal, India or a shop down the road.

Want to hear what you're aiming for first? Press play on the free singing bowl player, which loops a recording of my own bowl.

What You Need

  • A singing bowl. Any size works. Not sure which one suits you? Read how to choose a singing bowl size.
  • A striker (also called a mallet or puja). Plain wood gives a brighter sound and makes rim playing easy. Suede or felt gives a softer, warmer strike.
  • A cushion or ring (optional). It lets the bowl ring freely without sliding around.

Step 1: Hold the Bowl So It Can Ring

The bowl rings because its walls vibrate. Anything touching the walls stops them. So:

  • For a small bowl, rest it on the flat palm of your hand with your fingers straight, not curled up the sides.
  • For a medium or large bowl, set it on a cushion or ring on a table or the floor.
  • Sit comfortably with a straight back. Your shoulders should be relaxed, not hunched over the bowl.

Step 2: Strike It (the Easy Way)

Hold the striker like a pen, about halfway down. Tap the outside of the rim once, gently, with the striker at a slight angle. Then stop and listen. A good bowl will ring for 20 seconds or more, slowly fading.

Striking is the best way to start. It's how bowls are used to open and close a meditation, and it's hard to get wrong.

Step 3: Make It Sing by Circling the Rim

This is the part that gives the bowl its name. It feels tricky for about five minutes, then it suddenly clicks.

  1. Tap the rim once to wake the bowl up.
  2. Press the wooden side of the striker against the outside of the rim, keeping it upright.
  3. Move it slowly around the bowl with steady, even pressure. Think "stirring thick honey", not "scrubbing a pot".
  4. Use your whole arm, not just your wrist, and keep the speed the same all the way round.
  5. After two or three circles the tone builds into a continuous hum. Keep going for as long as you like.
  6. To finish, lift the striker away and listen until the sound disappears.

You can see me doing exactly this in the video on the home page: rimming first, then a few gentle strikes.

Fixing the Three Common Problems

"It rattles or buzzes"

You're going too fast, or the striker is bouncing. Slow right down and press a little more firmly so the striker stays in contact with the rim the whole way round.

"It won't build up to a hum"

Usually the pressure isn't even. Keep the striker upright and the pressure steady. A wooden striker builds sound faster than a suede one, so try the bare wood side.

"The sound dies straight away"

Something is touching the walls: fingers curled up the side, a cushion that's too deep, or the bowl sitting on a hard surface. Hold it on a flat palm and try again.

A Simple 5-Minute Singing Bowl Practice

Once you can get a clear sound, use it. Here's the practice I teach beginners:

  1. Sit and settle (1 minute). Close your eyes. Take three slow breaths, longer on the out-breath.
  2. Strike once and follow the sound (2 minutes). Listen to the tone from the moment it starts until you truly can't hear it any more. When it's gone, strike again. Your only job is to notice exactly when the sound ends.
  3. Rest in the quiet (1 minute). After the last strike fades, stay with the silence. Notice your breath and the feeling of your body sitting.
  4. Close (1 minute). Strike once more to finish. Open your eyes slowly.

That's it. Following a fading sound is one of the easiest ways into meditation, because it gives a busy mind one clear thing to do. For a longer version, read the full singing bowl meditation guide.

Looking After Your Bowl

  • Wipe it with a soft dry cloth after use. Fingerprints are fine, and many people like the natural patina.
  • Don't put water or anything else in it unless you're sure it's made for that.
  • Store it on its cushion, away from the edge of shelves. Dropping is the only real way to damage one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a singing bowl if I've never meditated?

Yes, and it's one of the best ways to start. The sound gives you a focus point so you're not just sitting with your thoughts. If you want guidance, the Meditation for Beginners community is made for exactly that.

How long should I play it for?

Five minutes a day beats an hour once a month. Start small and let it become a habit.

Is it OK to play it before bed?

Many people do. Keep it soft, with gentle strikes rather than loud rimming, and let the last sound fade before you lie down. You can also use our free player with a timer.
