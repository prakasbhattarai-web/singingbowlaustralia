Singing Bowl Sizes: How to Choose the Right One

Size changes the sound more than anything else. Here's how small, medium and large singing bowls differ, and how to pick one you'll actually use.

By Prakash Bhattarai · 14 November 2026 · 3 min read

Walk into any shop and you'll see singing bowls from the size of a teacup to the size of a mixing bowl. The good news: there's no "wrong" size. The right one depends on how and where you'll use it. This guide covers what each size sounds like, what it's good for, and how to spot a well-made bowl.

How Size Changes the Sound

The basic rule is the same as any instrument: bigger means deeper and longer. A larger bowl has more metal to vibrate, so it produces a lower note that rings for longer. A smaller bowl is brighter and higher, and fades sooner. Wall thickness, the shape of the rim and how the bowl was made also change the tone, which is why two bowls of the same size can sound quite different.

Small Singing Bowls (about 10–14 cm / 4–5.5 in)

  • Sound: bright and clear, shorter ring.
  • Best for: a desk or bedside table, travel, marking the start and end of a meditation, and first-time owners.
  • Good to know: small bowls sit on your palm, which makes them easy to learn with. Rim playing ("singing") takes a lighter touch.

Medium Singing Bowls (about 15–20 cm / 6–8 in)

  • Sound: warm and full, with a long ring.
  • Best for: daily home meditation. If you only buy one bowl, this is usually the one.
  • Good to know: it sings easily when you circle the rim and is still light enough to hold.

Large Singing Bowls (21 cm and up / 8+ in)

  • Sound: deep and rich, with a strong vibration you can feel.
  • Best for: sound baths, groups, yoga classes and practitioners.
  • Good to know: large bowls need a cushion and a soft (felt or suede) mallet. They're heavier and fill a room, which is lovely in a group but can be a lot in a small apartment.

Which Size Should You Choose?

  • "I want to start meditating at home." Choose medium.
  • "I want something small for my desk or to travel with." Choose small.
  • "I want something to help me unwind before sleep." Choose medium, played softly.
  • "I run classes or sound baths." Choose large, often alongside a small and a medium for a range of tones.

What to Look For in a Well-Made Bowl

  • A long, even ring. Strike it gently. A good bowl rings smoothly for 20 seconds or more without harsh rattles.
  • It sings easily. Circling the rim with a wooden striker should build up a hum within a few rounds.
  • Hand-hammered vs machine-made. Hand-hammered bowls show small dents from shaping and each one sounds unique. Machine-made bowls are smoother and more uniform. Both can sound good, so trust your ears.
  • Comes with a striker and cushion. You'll need both.
  • Honest description. Be wary of big claims about exact metal recipes or healing powers. A good seller tells you the size, weight, note and where it was made.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size singing bowl is best for beginners?

A medium bowl (about 15–20 cm) is easiest to play and suits daily meditation. A small bowl is a good budget or travel option.

Do I need more than one bowl?

No. One bowl is plenty for personal meditation. Sets of several sizes are mostly for sound baths and classes.

Which mallet should I use?

Bare wood for rim playing on small and medium bowls; suede or felt for softer strikes and for large bowls. See how to use a singing bowl for technique.
